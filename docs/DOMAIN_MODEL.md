# Exercise Director Domain Model

## Purpose

Exercise Director models the way experienced Exercise Directors think, so that every exercise is designed, delivered and assured with traceability, confidence and purpose.

The application models operational decision-making rather than simply reproducing exercise paperwork.

Every part of the system should support the journey from an identified training need to improved operational readiness.

---

## The Exercise

An Exercise is the main container within Exercise Director.

It brings together:

- Exercise identity
- Training demand
- Mission
- Training audience
- Starting state
- Desired finishing state
- Operational capability
- Collective Training Objectives
- Learning objectives
- Doctrine
- Resources
- Constraints
- Training opportunities
- Exercise design
- Scenario
- Master Events List
- Injects
- Assessments
- Evidence
- Assurance
- Lessons and improvement actions

The exercise provides the platform within which individuals and teams train.

The exercise itself is not the final product. Improved capability and preparedness are the intended outcomes.

---

## Exercise Definition

Before detailed design begins, the Exercise Director should establish:

- Exercise name
- Size and scale
- Training level
- Exercise type
- Location
- Dates and duration
- Available budget
- Units and organisations involved
- Training outcomes
- Starting state of the trainees
- Desired finishing state of the trainees
- Commander's additional training priorities
- Known constraints
- Planning assumptions
- Available resources
- Training opportunities

These details provide the context within which the exercise must be designed.

---

## Mission

The Mission explains why the exercise exists.

It should identify the operational purpose and the effect the training is intended to achieve.

The Mission provides direction for the capability, Collective Training Objectives and exercise design.

---

## Operational Capability

Capability describes what the training audience must be able to do in an operational context.

Examples include:

- Conduct an obstacle crossing
- Coordinate a multi-agency flood response
- Conduct joint fires
- Restore essential infrastructure
- Plan and control battlegroup operations

An exercise may have one primary capability and several supporting capabilities.

---

## Collective Training Objectives

Collective Training Objectives describe the tasks, behaviours and standards that teams, units or organisations must demonstrate.

A CTO may contain:

- Task
- Conditions
- Required standard
- Supporting actions
- Critical errors
- Success factors
- Measures and metrics
- Required evidence
- Relevant doctrine
- Level of challenge

CTOs may exist at different organisational levels.

For example:

- Exercise or formation CTO
- Unit CTO
- Sub-unit CTO
- Team or section CTO

Each lower-level CTO should contribute to the achievement of a higher-level collective outcome.

---

## Learning Objectives

Learning objectives describe what an individual or team should know, understand or be able to demonstrate.

Learning objectives should:

- support a Collective Training Objective;
- reflect the learner's role;
- be appropriate to the learner's starting state;
- be supported by doctrine;
- be measurable;
- be assessed through suitable training activity;
- contribute to the desired collective outcome.

Individual achievement does not automatically prove collective achievement. Evidence must be considered at the appropriate organisational level.

---

## Doctrine

Doctrine provides the authoritative basis for the training.

Doctrine may be held in:

- PDF documents
- Word documents
- SharePoint
- Local folders
- Government websites
- Military publication libraries
- Organisational policies and plans

Exercise Director should reference the authoritative source rather than assume ownership of the document.

Doctrine should explain why a task, standard or objective is included.

---

## Training Audience

The Training Audience identifies who is being prepared.

This may include:

- Individuals
- Teams
- Headquarters
- Units
- Multi-agency groups
- Strategic, operational or tactical command levels
- Supporting organisations

The design should take account of:

- Roles
- Experience
- Existing competence
- Previous training
- Known weaknesses
- Team composition
- Required operational responsibilities

---

## Starting State

The Starting State describes the current capability and preparedness of the training audience.

It provides the baseline against which progress can be judged.

The Starting State may include:

- Current qualifications
- Previous training
- Recent exercise performance
- Known gaps
- Confidence
- Team maturity
- Equipment familiarity
- Doctrinal understanding

---

## Desired Finishing State

The Desired Finishing State describes what should be different by the end of the exercise.

It should be expressed in terms of improved performance, capability, confidence or readiness.

The Desired Finishing State helps determine:

- CTOs
- Learning objectives
- Exercise challenge
- Assessment requirements
- Evidence requirements
- Follow-on training

---

## Commander's Priorities

Commander's Priorities identify additional areas of emphasis that should be incorporated into the exercise.

These may include:

- Specific tactical actions
- Leadership behaviours
- Communications
- Casualty management
- Information activities
- Integration with partners
- New equipment
- Recently identified weaknesses

Additional priorities should support rather than obscure the principal training purpose.

---

## Training Opportunities

Training Opportunities are activities, courses, organisations or resources that may be integrated to increase realism and training value.

Examples include:

- A Tank Driver or Commander course requiring final validation
- An Apache course requiring combined-arms integration
- An Engineer troop requiring bridge construction validation
- Another headquarters conducting a CPX
- Blue-light or government partners seeking compatible training
- New equipment requiring operational evaluation

A Training Opportunity may provide:

- Additional training audiences
- Equipment
- Vehicles
- Aircraft
- Specialist expertise
- Role players
- Training infrastructure
- Already-funded activity

Exercise Director should identify opportunities for mutual training benefit while leaving the final decision with the Exercise Director.

---

## Resources and Constraints

Resources determine what can realistically be delivered.

They may include:

- Budget
- Time
- Training areas
- Instructors and observers
- Equipment
- Vehicles
- Simulation
- Communications systems
- Role players
- HICON, LOCON and SIDECON
- Specialist support

Constraints may include:

- Safety
- Policy
- Security
- Environmental restrictions
- Estate limitations
- Availability
- Training level
- Exercise category
- Competing activity

Assurance should consider whether the exercise design is achievable with the resources available.

---

## Exercise Design

Exercise Design converts training requirements into a coherent learning experience.

It may include:

- Exercise structure
- Scenario
- Storyboard
- Exercise phases
- Training methods
- Level of challenge
- Resources
- Control arrangements
- Assessment strategy
- MEL/MIL
- Injects
- AAR arrangements

The design should remain centred on the learner while enabling collective performance to be observed.

---

## Scenario

The Scenario creates the operational context in which the training audience acts.

It should be:

- Relevant to the training need
- Appropriate to the level of challenge
- Doctrinally credible
- Realistic enough to create engagement
- Flexible enough to support learning
- Capable of producing the evidence required

The scenario exists to support the training, not overwhelm it.

---

## Master Events List

The Master Events List provides the planned exercise timeline.

It organises:

- Events
- Injects
- Phases
- Timings
- Sources
- Audiences
- Expected actions
- Control activity

The MEL should show how the design creates opportunities to assess the objectives and CTOs.

---

## Injects

Injects are controlled events or information introduced into the exercise.

An inject should exist for a clear reason.

It may:

- stimulate activity;
- create a decision;
- test a process;
- introduce friction;
- create an assessment opportunity;
- support scenario progression.

Injects are not objectives. They are mechanisms through which performance can be practised or observed.

---

## Assessment Strategy

The Assessment Strategy explains how performance will be observed and judged.

It should define:

- What will be assessed
- Who will assess it
- When assessment will occur
- What standard will be applied
- What evidence is required
- How individual and collective performance will be distinguished
- How assessments will contribute to CTO and capability judgements

---

## Assessment Records

Assessment Records capture professional observations during delivery.

A record may include:

- Inject
- Objective
- CTO
- Assessor
- Outcome
- Comments
- Time recorded
- Evidence
- Improvement action

Possible outcomes may include:

- Not assessed
- Achieved
- Partially achieved
- Not achieved

Professional judgement remains central.

---

## Evidence

Evidence supports conclusions about learning, performance and readiness.

Evidence may include:

- Instructor observations
- Trainee products
- Orders
- Reports and returns
- Decisions
- Timings
- Communications
- Simulation data
- Photographs
- Documents
- Recordings
- System-generated data

Evidence should be traceable to the objective or CTO it supports.

---

## Assurance

Assurance provides confidence that the exercise is coherent, traceable and suitable for its purpose.

Assurance may examine whether:

- The mission is clear
- The capability is defined
- CTOs support the capability
- Learning objectives support the CTOs
- Doctrine supports the objectives
- Injects create suitable assessment opportunities
- Evidence requirements are defined
- Resources support the design
- Constraints have been considered
- Assessment records support conclusions
- Lessons are captured

Assurance supports professional judgement. It does not replace it.

---

## Operational Readiness

Operational Readiness is the intended result of the training process.

Exercise Director should help demonstrate whether people and teams are better prepared to perform their operational roles.

Readiness should not be assumed solely because an exercise has been completed.

It should be supported by evidence.

---

## Organisational Learning

Every exercise should contribute to future improvement.

Organisational learning may include:

- Lessons identified
- Lessons learned
- Recommendations
- Improvement actions
- Recurring weaknesses
- Successful methods
- Doctrine review
- Future training requirements
- Changes to equipment, policy or procedures

The end of one exercise should inform the design of the next.

---

## The Golden Thread

The core traceability model is:

Mission

↓

Operational Capability

↓

Collective Training Objectives

↓

Learning Objectives

↓

Doctrine and Assessment Strategy

↓

Exercise Design

↓

Scenario and Master Events List

↓

Injects and Training Activity

↓

Assessment Records

↓

Evidence

↓

Assurance

↓

Operational Readiness

↓

Organisational Learning

↓

Future Training Design

The thread should be traceable in both directions.

An Exercise Director should be able to explain why every major design element exists and what evidence supports each conclusion.

---

## Core Principle

**Exercise Director does not exist merely to manage exercises. It exists to strengthen and support professional judgement in the design, delivery and assurance of training that prepares people and teams for operations.**

---

**Better prepared today. Stronger tomorrow.**
