What is the Craft?

The craft of an Exercise Director is the ability to design, deliver and evaluate learning that prepares people and organisations for operations.

It combines professional judgement, operational understanding and instructional skill.

An Exercise Director develops people.

Exercises are simply one of the methods available.

The Responsibilities of an Exercise Director

A professional Exercise Director...

Understands the operational requirement.
Defines the readiness required.
Identifies the readiness gap.
Designs learning to close that gap.
Creates a safe learning environment.
Collects honest evidence.
Supports professional judgement.
Enables continuous improvement.
Safety

Training must always be safe.

Operations may never be.

One of the greatest responsibilities of an Exercise Director is creating a safe environment in which people can prepare for an unsafe environment.

Safety enables learning.

It must never remove realism.

Professional Judgement

Professional judgement cannot be automated.

Exercise Director supports judgement.

It never replaces it.

Evidence informs judgement.

Experience gives judgement value.

Expertise

An Exercise Director is not expected to know everything.

They bring together the right expertise.

Subject Matter Experts provide technical knowledge.

Exercise Directors create the conditions in which that expertise becomes learning.

Intervention

One of the greatest skills of an Exercise Director is knowing when to intervene.

Intervene too early...

...learning is lost.

Intervene too late...

...safety may be compromised.

Professional judgement balances both.

Honest Evidence

Evidence exists to support learning.

Evidence must never be manipulated to produce comfortable conclusions.

Learning depends upon honesty.

Operational readiness depends upon learning.

Success

A successful exercise is not one that runs perfectly.

A successful exercise develops operational readiness.

Sometimes the greatest learning comes from things that did not go according to plan.

Mission Command accepts uncertainty.

Training should prepare people for it.

Legacy

Every experienced Exercise Director carries knowledge that has often taken decades to acquire.

Exercise Director exists to preserve that knowledge and help pass it to future generations.

Technology changes.

The craft must endure.
