13 - THE PROFESSIONAL LANGUAGE LAYER
Principle

Exercise Director thinks in one language but speaks in many.

The underlying readiness method never changes.

The language presented to the user adapts to the profession, organisation and role.

The software performs the translation.

The user never should.

Why

Professionals should immediately recognise their own world.

An Infantry Battalion 2IC should feel the software understands Battalion training.

A Police Silver Commander should feel it understands policing.

An Emergency Planner should feel it understands resilience.

The questions remain the same.

Only the language changes.

The Method

The Apprentice always seeks to understand:

Why are we preparing?
Who are we preparing?
What must they be able to do?
What currently prevents success?
How will we recognise success?
What evidence supports that judgement?

These questions never change.

The Voice

Examples

British Army

What mission are you preparing your unit to conduct?

Emergency Planning

What capability are you preparing your organisation to deliver?

Fire & Rescue

What type of incident are your crews preparing to respond to?

Police

What operational capability are your officers preparing to deliver?

NHS

What clinical response is your team preparing to provide?

Design Rule

Never ask the user to learn our language.

Always learn theirs.

Philosophy

People trust software that sounds like someone who understands their profession.

Exercise Director should always feel like an experienced colleague.

Never a generic application.

Our Promise
