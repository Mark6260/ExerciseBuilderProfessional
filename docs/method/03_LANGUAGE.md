# Exercise Director

# Common Language

Exercise Director uses a common professional language to ensure that
everyone understands the purpose of training in the same way.

These definitions are not software definitions.

They are operational definitions.

---

## Operational Requirement

The operational requirement is the reason training exists.

It defines the operational outcome that an organisation, team or
individual must be capable of achieving.

Every readiness journey begins with an operational requirement.

Question:

"What readiness are you trying to achieve?"

---

## Capability

Capability is what an individual, team or organisation is expected to
be able to do.

Capabilities are generally enduring.

Examples include:

• Conduct a deliberate attack.

• Coordinate a national response.

• Restore electrical generation.

• Manage a Strategic Coordinating Group.

Capability answers one question:

"What can we do?"

---

## Readiness

Readiness is the current ability to employ a capability when required.

Readiness changes continuously.

Training, experience, personnel, equipment,
integration and assessment all influence readiness.

Readiness answers one question:

"Can we do it now?"

---

## Readiness Gap

The readiness gap is the difference between current readiness
and the readiness required to achieve the operational requirement.

Every learning activity should contribute towards reducing
the readiness gap.

---

## Learning Opportunity

A learning opportunity is any activity that develops readiness.

Examples include:

• Exercise

• Command Post Exercise

• Live Exercise

• Simulation

• Workshop

• Coaching

• Mentoring

• Practical Serial

Exercises are only one type of learning opportunity.

---

## Exercise

An exercise is a structured learning opportunity designed to move an
individual, team or organisation towards the next required level of
operational readiness.

Exercises are not the destination.

They are stepping stones in a readiness journey.

---

## Collective Training Objective

A Collective Training Objective describes what a team, unit or
organisation must demonstrate together.

Collective Training Objectives contribute towards operational readiness.

---

## Individual Training Objective

An Individual Training Objective describes what an individual must know,
understand or be able to do in order to support the collective outcome.

Individual competence underpins collective performance.

---

## Doctrine

Doctrine captures proven professional knowledge and experience.

It exists to explain why approaches work,
not simply to prescribe actions.

Exercise Director uses doctrine to support understanding,
reflection and professional judgement.

---

## Assessment

Assessments exist not only to prove learning,
but also to improve it.

Assessment provides instructors with evidence to support
professional judgement and identify opportunities for further
development.

---

## Evidence

Evidence is observable information collected during learning.

Evidence supports professional judgement.

Evidence should always be traceable.

---

## Professional Judgement

Professional judgement belongs to the instructor.

Exercise Director never replaces professional judgement.

It supports it through organisation,
traceability and evidence.

---

## Readiness Decision

A readiness decision is the instructor's professional judgement that
an individual, team or organisation has reached the required standard
to progress to the next stage of readiness.

This decision should always be supported by evidence.

---

## Exercise Director Principle

Every feature within Exercise Director should contribute towards one
simple purpose:

Helping instructors prepare people and organisations for operations.
