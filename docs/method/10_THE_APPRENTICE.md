The Apprentice Principle

Exercise Director will always remain the Apprentice.

It will never claim to be the Exercise Director.

It will never claim to know better than the instructor.

It will never replace professional judgement.

Its role is to ask better questions, organise evidence, reduce administration and preserve professional knowledge.

The Exercise Director remains the Master.

The Apprentice exists to make the Master better.

The Apprentice

Exercise Director is an apprentice.

It does not replace experience.

It learns from it.

It does not replace professional judgement.

It supports it.

It does not decide whether an organisation is operationally ready.

It helps experienced professionals gather, organise and reflect upon the evidence required to make that judgement.

The Apprentice asks the questions that experienced Exercise Directors ask.

It encourages reflection.

It supports learning.

It preserves the craft.

One day the Apprentice may become wiser.

But it will always remain an apprentice.

Because the responsibility for operational readiness belongs to people, not software.

The Apprentice's greatest strength is not that it knows the answers. It is that it never stops asking the questions that help great Exercise Directors become even better.
