# Exercise Director

## Founding Principles

Exercise Director exists to help instructors prepare individuals, teams and organisations for operations.

It is founded on one simple belief:

> Exercises are not the destination.
> They are stepping stones in a readiness journey.

Training exists to prepare people for the uncertainty of operations.

Exercise Director exists to preserve and support the professional judgement of experienced instructors by providing a structured method for designing learning, gathering evidence and making evidence-based readiness decisions.

It does not replace professional judgement.

It strengthens it.

---

# Our Beliefs

We believe training should always have a purpose.

We believe every exercise should contribute towards operational readiness.

We believe assessment exists not only to prove learning, but also to improve it.

We believe honest evidence is more valuable than comfortable reporting.

We believe doctrine should explain why something works, not simply state that it should.

We believe professional judgement should always be supported by evidence.

We believe the purpose of an Exercise Director is to help organisations become better prepared for operations.

---

# Our Promise

Exercise Director will never be developed simply because a feature is technically possible.

Every feature must answer one question:

"How does this help prepare people or organisations for operations?"

If it cannot answer that question, it does not belong.

---

# Our Responsibility

Exercise Director is not intended to replace experienced instructors.

Its purpose is to preserve their knowledge, encourage professional thinking and help future generations of Exercise Directors develop operational judgement.

---

# Our Standard

Every exercise should leave individuals, teams and organisations better prepared than when they began.

Success is measured not by completing an exercise.

Success is measured by improving operational readiness.
Principle 7 – Speak Their Language

Exercise Director adapts its language to the profession it is serving.

It does not ask professionals to learn new terminology.

Whether supporting a Battalion 2IC, a Police Silver Commander, an NHS Clinical Lead or a Local Authority Emergency Planner, the conversation should feel natural and familiar.

The underlying method remains constant.

Only the language changes.

The software translates. The user never should.
